Product manager at Zynga. From Tamil Nadu, in Bangalore.

I grew up in Tamil Nadu and live in Bangalore with my family. I studied mechanical engineering, which is a roundabout way of saying I really wanted to solve problems — and eventually found a job where that was the whole point.

These days I work on live‑ops for a mobile racing game: the events, the rewards, and the endless question of what to run next and when. It is a strange job to explain at a dinner party, and I have stopped trying to make it sound normal.

Outside of work I referee football, which I am qualified to do and about which I have strong and mostly unpopular opinions. I host quizzes. I do the food at family weddings, which is a tradition rather than a talent.

I am a cyclist, an amateur cook, and a reliably bad chess player. I like long walks, long flights, and getting a bit lost in a new city.

Ask me about Tamil food, football, or why live‑ops is harder than it sounds.

Aravind Vasudevan.

Product manager — games, data, and live operations.

I’m a product manager at Zynga, working on live‑ops for a large mobile racing title. I own the roadmap for the events and features that ship into that game, and I’m responsible for the revenue they generate.

My work sits mostly in the unglamorous middle of the job: deciding what to ship and when, building the models that tell us whether it worked, and making sure the answer reaches the people who can act on it. I’d rather build the framework than the deck.

Before Zynga I was at Capillary Technologies, in product management and analytics for brands running loyalty and retention programmes. Before that I was a business analyst at Analytics Quotient, working on marketing analytics for retail clients.

Somewhere in there I did an MBA at ISB, having started out as a mechanical engineer. In hindsight that detour is the most useful thing I’ve done — it’s what turned a job I was good at into one I actually wanted.

I care about products that earn their retention, about measurement that changes a decision rather than just describing one, and about teams that argue about the work instead of about who’s right. I write about these things occasionally, and I build small tools when I want to understand something properly.
